# Upgrade authority

<p className="lede">
  During the loan, the protocol holds upgrade authority for your program. Repay the loan and authority moves to your wallet. Default and the program is closed.
</p>

<Callout kind="tldr">
  **tl;dr —** During the loan, `authority_pda` (PDA seeds `["authority"]`) is the upgrade authority. On `repay_loan`, the deployer service calls `transfer_authority_to_borrower(loan_id)` to move authority into your wallet.
</Callout>

## Why escrow

Authority is the collateral. The protocol fronts the rent for your program data account; if it didn't hold authority, you could walk away with a deployed program funded by LP capital. With authority, the protocol can close the program at expiry and reclaim the rent for LPs.

A few consequences flow from this:

- **Only one authority at a time.** The BPF Upgradeable Loader supports a single upgrade authority. While that's the escrow PDA, it's not you.
- **The escrow PDA is program-owned.** No human signs for it. The Solignition program signs via PDA seeds when it needs to (e.g. transferring authority to you on repay, or closing the program on default).
- **Authority isn't transferable mid-loan.** There's no instruction to hand authority to a third party while the loan is open.

## The two-step handoff on repay

Repay isn't atomic with the authority transfer. Two distinct on-chain steps:

1. **`repay_loan(loan_id)`** — you sign. Principal + interest paid. State moves from `Active` to `RepaidPendingTransfer`. Upgrade authority still sits in the escrow PDA.
2. **`transfer_authority_to_borrower(loan_id)`** — the deployer signs. CPIs into the BPF Upgradeable Loader to set you as upgrade authority. State moves from `RepaidPendingTransfer` to `Repaid`.

The split exists because the BPF Upgradeable Loader requires the current authority to sign the change — and only the protocol can sign for the escrow PDA. The deployer service watches for `LoanRepaid` events and runs step 2 automatically.

In normal operation step 2 happens within seconds of step 1. The CLI's `solignition status <id>` will show you when it completes.

## During the loan

**Upgrades are not supported.** The protocol does not implement a "push an upgrade through the escrow" flow. If you need to ship a new version, you have two choices:

- **Wait until repay**, then upgrade normally with your authority.
- **Repay early**, upgrade, request a new loan.

Because interest is linear and time-prorated (see [Interest math](/protocol/interest)), repaying early just costs less interest. There's no penalty.

Plan accordingly: pick a loan duration that matches how stable you expect the deployed version to be.

## After repay

Once state is `Repaid`, you hold standard BPF upgrade authority on `program_pubkey`. From there it's vanilla Solana:

- **Upgrade:** `solana program deploy --upgrade-authority ./me.json --program-id <programPubkey> new_version.so`.
- **Rotate authority:** `solana program set-upgrade-authority <programPubkey> --new-upgrade-authority <newAuthority>`.
- **Freeze (make immutable):** `solana program set-upgrade-authority <programPubkey> --final`. **Irreversible.**
- **Close (recover rent):** `solana program close <programPubkey> --recipient <walletPubkey>`. Also **irreversible**.

The protocol has no further claim on the program after `Repaid`.

## Common questions

**Can I see the escrow PDA address?** Yes — it's deterministic. Derive it with `findProgramAddressSync([Buffer.from("authority")], programId)`.

**What if the deployer service is slow on step 2?** The protocol state will sit in `RepaidPendingTransfer`. The deployer is monitored; if it stalls, you can reach out via [Discord](https://discord.gg/7yBEb7GUee). Your repay is already done — the SOL is in the vault — so you're not paying additional interest.

**Can the admin force a transfer?** No. Only the deployer key can call `transfer_authority_to_borrower`. The admin can rotate the deployer key via `update_config` if the current deployer is unresponsive.
